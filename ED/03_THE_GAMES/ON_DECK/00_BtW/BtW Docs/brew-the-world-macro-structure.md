# BREW the WORLD — Macro Narrative Structure

*Working reference document. High-altitude only — acts and major turn points, not scene-by-scene. Companion to the cast bible and style bible.*

---

## Structural Logic

Six acts, built as two acts per movement of the core loop — Harvest, Brew, Sell — rather than three even acts. This keeps the loop's own thematic architecture (harvest → brew → sell, the title's own pun) as the backbone while giving each movement room to actually breathe across two acts instead of being flattened into one.

---

## Act 1 — Harvest I: The Surface

Pure skeleton-building. Establish the shop, the family, Fennel's generation, and the wider village at full cozy strength — Orchard presence ambient and unremarked, exactly as warm as the color language and environment sections describe. No swings trigger here. This act's job is to make the diorama-pretty normalcy real enough that its later cracking actually costs something.

**Closing beat:** a small, deniable unease — Weasel's leniency toward Fennel reads as slightly odd to an attentive player, with no explanation offered yet.

---

## Act 2 — Harvest II: First Weight

The player starts actually *seeing* what Act 1 normalized — the requisition stamps, the placard by the door, the paperwork that's just paperwork until it isn't. Introduce Mugwort's garden as the one Orchard-free space in the world, the visible "third path." Barley enters as an enforcer who genuinely believes what he does is mercy — not yet tested.

**Closing beat — the Harvest movement's climax:** an unmistakably war-adjacent order comes through the counter, and Fennel has to actually brew it and hand it over. Not inference anymore. The first non-deniable weight.

---

## Act 3 — Brew I: Heat Rising

Complications compound. Lower-stakes swings start trending without fully resolving — Cricket takes bigger risks on air as Owl keeps feeding him fragments of truth; the pressure on Sparrow to mark someone starts becoming concrete rather than hypothetical; Weasel reveals more of what the ledgers actually represent. This act is relationships under rising pressure, not revelations.

---

## Act 4 — Brew II: The Old Recipe

The centerpiece act. The Hemlock/Pine reveal lands here — village co-founder, forgotten hero, living doctrine — surfaced through Owl and Marigold. The village-square monument's lighting arc reaches its "hard to ignore" phase. Cedar's own recruitment (the bedside skirmish, Hemlock's first finished work) comes to light. Fox learns, or nearly learns, the truth about Lemon. Barley's crux with Lemon comes to a head.

**Closing beat — the Brew movement's climax:** Kirkwood's broadcast swing. Not "Fennel realizes" — the actual mechanical and narrative pivot already built into his tarot card (Judgement: wakes people vs. lulls them). Whether he names the supply chain on air is what forces the shop's secret from ambient to public, for the whole village, not just the player. This is the hinge the entire back half depends on.

---

## Act 5 — Sell I: Consequences

Immediate fallout from Kirkwood's choice. The village reacts, Hemlock reacts, the Orchard tightens or the resistance emboldens depending on what went public. Remaining swings resolve here: Ash's final lean (especially if Badger's secret hasn't triggered it yet), Sparrow's mark-or-choose moment with Robin, Weasel's final surface-or-bury call on the ledger. The coalition — or its absence — becomes visible: how many of the swing-capable characters actually tipped toward unity is legible to the player for the first time.

---

## Act 6 — Sell II: The Reckoning

Endgame. Hemlock and Fennel, direct — his ambition to finish what he started with Cedar, tested against everything the player has built or failed to build across the first five acts. Multiple endings, gated by the aggregate of accumulated swing outcomes, not a single final choice. Per the standing design rule: the "successful" ending — shop thriving, full absorption, Fennel completing what Cedar became — is explicitly the worst one. The real win looks smaller: the coalition holds, unity chosen over the Orchard's manufactured crisis, nothing flashy about it. Coda resolves what's left of Fennel's family relationships based on everything accumulated, not a single epilogue slide.
