## CHAPTER THREE — THE PROMPT IN THE FOG

| Timestamp / Chapter | Visual / B-Roll Execution Guide | Narrative Voiceover Script |
|---|---|---|
| **7:00 – 7:45**<br>**The Translucent Prompt** | Close-up capture of a console/interact prompt in a dim Grineer galleon corridor — thin, low-contrast white text over a busy metallic background. Simulate astigmatism blur with a mild post-processing filter (clearly labeled on-screen as "simulated visual condition") to show how much worse it reads. | *"This is an interact prompt. It's asking you to hack a console mid-mission, under time pressure, often while enemies are still active nearby. Watch what happens when we simulate even a mild case of astigmatism — a condition that affects an estimated one in three adults to some degree." (show blurred version) "The text doesn't just get harder to read. It nearly disappears into the environment it's floating over."* |
| **7:45 – 8:30**<br>**Pattern Repetition** | Quick montage: three or four different prompt instances across different tilesets (ice, corpus ship, grineer asteroid) — all sharing the same thin, floating, low-contrast text treatment. | *"This isn't a one-off oversight. It's a consistent design language across the entire early game — thin sans-serif text, no background plate, no outline weight, rendered directly over whatever chaotic environment happens to be behind it. For players without visual impairments, it's a minor friction. For a meaningful percentage of the player base, it's a genuine accessibility wall, and it hits hardest during the exact tutorial moments meant to teach core interaction loops."* |
| **8:30 – 9:00**<br>**The Stakes** | Cut to a montage of other UI moments where Warframe *does* get this right — mission complete screens, codex entries with solid dark plates behind text. | *"The frustrating part is that Warframe already knows the solution. Look at the mission summary screen — solid plates, high contrast, generous weight. The technology and the taste already exist inside this game. They simply haven't been applied to the moments where a brand-new player is leaning in closest, trying hardest to learn."* |

## CHAPTER FOUR — THE HALLWAY LIED TO YOU

| Timestamp / Chapter | Visual / B-Roll Execution Guide | Narrative Voiceover Script |
|---|---|---|
| **9:00 – 9:50**<br>**The Sterile Tutorial** | Full capture of the bullet-jump tutorial sequence — clean, well-lit, geometrically simple hallway. Show the player nailing every prompt in sequence, feeling instantly competent. | *"This is the bullet-jump tutorial. It's genuinely well made in isolation — a clean hallway, unambiguous prompts, generous margins for error. Within ninety seconds, a new player feels like they understand movement. And then the tutorial ends, and Earth begins."* |
| **9:50 – 10:45**<br>**The Reality of Earth** | Hard cut to an actual Earth tileset — Grineer Forest — overgrown, visually dense foliage, ambiguous verticality, outdated geometry with unclear collision silhouettes. Show the player fumbling: bullet-jumping into a tree canopy, misjudging a ledge, getting caught on foliage clutter. | *"Earth's Grineer Forest tileset is, by Warframe's own current art standards, one of the oldest environments in the game. It's visually dense in ways that actively fight the movement system the tutorial just taught. Foliage silhouettes read identically to solid geometry. Verticality is ambiguous — is that a ledge you can grapple to, or set dressing? The hallway taught a grammar. Earth speaks a different, older dialect."* |
| **10:45 – 11:30**<br>**The Compounding Failure** | Side-by-side: tutorial hallway movement (fluid, confident) vs. Earth movement (hesitant, corrective). Overlay input-display (keys pressed) to show the new player's hesitation and re-tries. | *"This is the compounding failure of tutorial design: it's not enough to teach a mechanic in a vacuum. You have to teach it in an environment that resembles where the player will actually use it. Right now, Warframe teaches bullet-jumping in a museum and then asks players to use it in a jungle that predates the mechanic's own final form. The lesson and the exam don't match."* |
| **11:30 – 12:00**<br>**Transition** | Slow pull-back shot revealing the full scale of an Earth tile from above — genuinely striking, but visually overwhelming for a first-hour player. | *"None of this is a case against Earth's art direction on its own terms. It's a case that the *order of operations* — teach in the simple space, then immediately test in the most visually complex space in the early game — has the sequencing backwards."* |

Story Mode clears the noise floor entirely — no fissures, no syndicate icons, no alerts — leaving only the critical path lit, exactly as new players need it, and exactly as veterans replaying the intro on a fresh account would appreciate. Operations Mode restores the full, dense, information-rich view that veterans have earned the fluency to read. This isn't about removing systems. It's about acknowledging that a map serves two entirely different audiences, and building two views instead of forcing one."* |
| **16:10 – 17:00**<br>**Proposal 2: The High-Contrast Prompt Box** | Design mockup: take the earlier low-contrast prompt example and redesign it live in the edit — add a solid dark plate, increase font weight, add a subtle outline. A solid, semi-opaque bounding plate behind every interact and objective prompt, paired with increased font weight. This is a small visual change with an outsized accessibility return — and, notably, it's a treatment Warframe already uses successfully elsewhere in its own UI. This isn't invention. It's consistency."* |
| **17:00 – 18:00**<br>**Proposal 3: The Flow State Agility Course** | Concept sketch or simple whiteboard-style diagram of an optional Codex-accessible movement course — momentum gates, timed rings, escalating verticality — styled after Warframe's own Simulacrum tech. | *"Proposal three: The Flow State Agility Course. An optional, entirely skippable movement gauntlet, accessed through the Codex — built on the same simulation technology already powering the Simulacrum. Momentum gates, timed checkpoints, escalating verticality, all in a controlled space with none of Earth's visual noise. This bridges the gap the tutorial hallway currently can't: a space complex enough to build real competence, but still legible enough to teach it safely."* |
| **18:00 – 19:15**<br>**Proposal 4: The Resonance Bow System** | Concept animation/mockup: end-of-mission summary screen with a new prompt allowing players to select a teammate and trigger a physical Tenno bow animation, with categorized honors ("Combat," "Fashion," "Guidance"). Show a mockup Relay scene where a player's Warframe has a subtle visual "ripple" aesthetic effect tied to accumulated honors. | *"Proposal four, and the one closest to Warframe's soul: The Resonance Bow System. At mission end, players may select a squadmate and offer a physical bow — Warframe already has the animation vocabulary for this kind of reverence — categorized by what earned it: combat excellence, fashion-frame artistry, or guidance given to a newer player. Accumulated honors manifest as subtle aesthetic ripples visible on your Warframe in Relays — nothing pay-to-win, nothing mechanically loud, just a quiet, visible record of a community recognizing itself. Warframe's social fabric is already its strongest asset. This gives it a formal grammar."* |
| **19:15 – 19:45**<br>**Blueprint Recap** | Fast montage recap: all four proposals shown in quick succession with clean text labels. | *"Four proposals. A map that speaks two languages instead of one. Text you can actually read under pressure. A training ground that matches the exam. And a system that turns respect into something visible. None of it changes what Warframe is. All of it changes how quickly new players get to feel what Warframe is."* |

## PRODUCTION NOTES

- **Capture priorities:** Fresh account footage (Chapters 1, 2, 4, 5) must come from an actual new account, not a veteran account with UI reset — visual noise density differs meaningfully by progression state.
- **Astigmatism simulation (Ch. 3):** Use a legitimate accessibility-simulation filter (not exaggerated blur) and caption it clearly as a simulation to maintain credibility with the accessibility community.
- **Music bed:** License or compose in the register of Warframe's own ambient menu themes — pads, low percussion, restrained. Avoid hype-trailer music; the tone is retrospective, not promotional.
- **On-screen citations:** Any community sentiment referenced (Ch. 5) should be paraphrased composites, not verbatim quoted posts, to avoid misattribution.
- **Runtime pacing check:** Chapters 1–5 (diagnosis) should not exceed 12:00 total. Chapter 6 (blueprint) is the emotional payoff — protect its length in the edit even if earlier chapters need trimming.
