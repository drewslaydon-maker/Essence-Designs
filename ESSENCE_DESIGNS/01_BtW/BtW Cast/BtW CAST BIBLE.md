# BREW the WORLD — Cast Bible & Chain of Connection

*Working reference document. Compiled from ongoing design sessions.*

## Title & World Logic

**Title:** *BREW the WORLD* — deliberate double-read. "Brew the world" (make/concoct) with "save the world" ghosted underneath, unspoken. The gap between the title's implied promise and what the game actually delivers stays implicit — never spelled out in-game.

**Nation:** The Fields
**Ruling party:** The Orchard
**Resistance:** The Weeds (a dismissive Orchard term, reclaimed)
**The war:** "The Last War" — Orchard propaganda framing (the war that ends war, oxymoronic and self-aware). Lands, weapons, and specifics are deliberately never named — the Orchard's entire point is that the specifics don't matter, only the war's continued existence does.
**Magic:** Rare, belief-powered force usable by either side.

**Naming convention:** Botanical names are reserved for family and major narrative-carrying characters. Everyone else uses a food / animal / elemental scheme — a naming signal that tells an attentive player who's carrying real weight before the story does.

**Governing thesis:** The Orchard sells the player a false, singular crisis — the war, framed as inevitable and un-endable. The true path available to the player the entire time is unglamorous: talk to, respect, and fight for the people who make up and sustain the village. Some outside "hemmer and locker" doesn't get to decide that for them. Unity, not the war, is the actual answer the game is hiding in plain sight — the Orchard's "inevitable victory" framing only holds if the player never notices what's already structurally connected around them.

**The Hollows:** Victims of the Orchard's Picker process. The process takes their voice — the Hollows cannot speak — and visibly disfigures them past casual recognition as human. This is the game's core monster-encounter type. Their humanity isn't legible on sight; recovering it is work the player has to do, the same gap the governing thesis asks the player to close everywhere else. Three angles on the same mechanic: Barley makes them (institutional, dutiful — his one broken protocol was pulling Lemon out before the process finished), Lemon is one, named and individuated (the personal, spared exception), and Fox has killed them without knowing — or without letting himself know — what they used to be (the unwitting outsider, violence at a remove).

## The Chain of Power (top-down)

**THE ORCHARD** (the state)
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;↓
**HEMLOCK** — former teacher/scholar, now the doctrine's living enforcement. Shaped Fennel's gift from a distance, years before she could place him. Wants total, willing devotion — completion, not mere obedience.
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;↓ (chain-of-command — confirmed for Cedar, recruited in person; still unconfirmed for Barley and Kirkwood)
**CEDAR** (quartermaster) · **BARLEY** (Picker enforcer) · **KIRKWOOD** (if state-ward)
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;↓
**THE VILLAGE** — every remaining character either serves this chain, resists it, or is caught in the middle of it without knowing which.

## The Chain of Resistance (bottom-up)

**SAGE** (active Weeds operative) ↔ **MARIGOLD** (cover, community anchor, web crux)
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;↓
**OWL** (hides texts) · **SPARROW** (smuggles messages) · **FLINT** (hides people/goods) · **BADGER** (repairs under cover) · **LOAM** (skims yield)
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;↓
**FOX** (reciprocity-based outsider) · swing-capable: **WEASEL, CRICKET, KIRKWOOD, CEDAR, BARLEY**

**FENNEL** sits at the convergence of both chains — daughter of a state functionary and an operative, unknowingly cultivated by the man at the top of the state chain, in daily contact with nearly every node in the resistance chain through the shop counter. She is the point where "inevitable" and "assemblable" meet.

## Generations

**The Trifecta (elder)** — Pine (now Hemlock), Owl, Marigold. Founded the village together. Pine's departure — the transformation into the doctrine, into Hemlock — is the fracture that broke the trio, and then broke Owl and Marigold's own marriage on top of it: Owl turned to his books, trying to hold what was lost by cataloguing it; Marigold turned to the community, trying to replace what was lost by holding everyone else together instead. Two different answers to the same wound, incompatible enough to end a marriage even between two people who'd both stayed loyal to each other and to the village.

**The Established (middle)** — Cedar, Sage, confirmed anchors of this tier: married across the state/resistance divide, the origin tension the whole family sits on. Badger and Nessa belong here too — married, same generation, already load-bearing before Fennel's story starts. Stag, Loam, and Weasel are now confirmed here as well: each carries a relationship to the founding generation or the Established couples that predates Fennel entirely (Stag as Cedar's old comrade, Weasel as Sage's past, Loam's provision role already established before Fennel took over the shop). Butter is deliberately left out of this tier — nothing ties him to the founding era; he reads as a contemporary of Fennel's own generation who simply took the easier path early.

**Fennel's Generation** — Fennel, Kirkwood, Clover, Daisy, Mugwort, Fox, Coal, Ash, Ember, and likely Cricket and Sparrow by age. Butter sits here too, by elimination from the Established tier above. Mostly unaware of the world before Fennel, or aware only in fragments passed down by the Trifecta and Established tiers. Mugwort belongs here specifically as ongoing, not historical, proof that Pine/Hemlock's doctrine still can't replicate what she does — his failure isn't a past event, it's still failing in real time, generation after generation.

- Badger ↔ Nessa — married; craft and sea, two trades under the same quota pressure. Nessa ferries what Badger forges, disguised as catch — his craft and her boat are one operation split across two trades. Ash and Ember are theirs
- Ash ↔ Barley — mirror risk: what Ash could become if he leans Picker
- Ash ↔ Badger — if he ever learns what his father actually forges under cover, that discovery is the swing trigger itself: report it and become the mirror in the line above, bury it and he's already Weeds
- Coal ↔ Fennel — peer friendship, Fennel's generation
- Ember ↔ Coal — not blood-siblings, different mother, but Ember treats him as hers to protect anyway (Emporio/Jolyne register — more caretaker than peer)
- Ember ↔ Ash — she suspects his lean before it's spoken aloud
- Fennel ↔ Kirkwood — her shop is the literal supply engine for the war he served and returned changed from; neither of them has said this out loud yet — the material his broadcast swing eventually forces into the open, one way or the other

**Key structural insight:** Recounted against the full cast as it stands now (25 named characters, Mugwort excluded as the deliberate exception below): **16 of 25** carry an active Weeds tie or a plausible sphere-swing lean toward one — up since the original tally, as Nessa, Robin, Weasel, Cricket, and Ash have all picked up ties in recent passes. Only **Hemlock and Butter** now read as purely, uncomplicated Orchard-loyal with no swing potential at all. Weasel no longer belongs in that "purely loyal" bucket he was originally filed under — he's tagged sphere-swing in the diagram and already carries a canon act of leniency toward Fennel, so his original inclusion there was inconsistent with his own entry. Worth resolving directly. The coalition the game needs already exists in the web — it just isn't assembled yet.

**Mugwort is the 20th character and the deliberate exception to that insight** — she carries no Weeds tie and no Orchard tie. She sits entirely outside both chains by design, which is itself the point: unity doesn't require enlistment in either structure, and her belief-power proves that a third path — neither state doctrine nor organized resistance — was viable the whole time.
